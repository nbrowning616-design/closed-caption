How to play

One caption is real. Everyone else is lying.

Ranked like chess, but judged by friends.

Playing over FaceTime or Zoom? Works great — everyone uses their own phone.

Everyone gets the same cartoon, no caption. The game knows the real one. Write a fake that could pass for it — then bet chips on which caption is real.

Each round

1
Everyone writes. The dealer too. Hate the cartoon? Vote to skip it.
2
One goes face-down. The game turns one caption over: the Closed Caption. Usually a player’s. The real one about one round in seven.
3
Blind bets. The dealer reads the open ones aloud. Then everyone bets at once, in secret: 1 or 2 chips on any caption — the face-down one too. The chips drop together. The dealer doesn’t bet.
4
The steal. Before the flip, the dealer takes one chip off the table. Off the real one = its points go to the dealer. Off a fake, its owner gets +1: caught you.
5
The flip. The Closed Caption turns over. Anyone with a chip left puts it down, in turn from the dealer’s left.
6
The reveal. One caption at a time, every few seconds (the dealer can hold it): who wrote it, who fell for it. The real one comes last. Points are paid and the deal moves left.

Scoring

The extras ▾
  • Frame It. On the reveal, tap Frame on any caption that beat the real one (never your own). Frame as many as deserve it. When most of the other players frame the same one, it hangs in The Gallery (menu ☰) for good. At the end of the night, the table votes one Caption of the Night.
  • Ha and Groan. Two quiet buttons on every caption, from the reading on. No points. Nobody sees a count until the answer. A caption with no chips and three or more Groans gets a rejection slip from The Editors (menu ☰, Rejections).
  • Call your shot. Once your caption is in, name the one person you think will fall for it. If they put a chip on it, +3. The reveal says how it went either way.
  • The table vs. the cartoonist. Ha’s tapped before anyone knows which caption is real are a blind test: the table’s best fake against the real one, one point a round.
  • The Late Edition. In a game that goes around the table more than once, the last trip around pays double. First to 20: when someone gets there, the trip around the table is finished (everyone deals the same number of times), at double points. Then the leader wins.
  • A tie for first goes to whoever fooled the most people. Still level: more captions framed. Still level: a tie.
  • The Remote. Every table has one: a 1980s TV remote with one button, held by the last winner. Each winner adds a strip of masking tape with their name and the date.
  • The credits. The night ends like a movie: a freeze-frame on each player, and what became of them.
  • Your rating. Ranked like chess, but judged by friends. Everyone starts at 1,200; after each game you gain points from everyone you finished above and lose them to everyone who finished above you. Beating higher-rated players moves it more; a short game moves it less. Titles: Candidate 1,400, Master 1,600, Senior Master 1,800, Grandmaster 2,000. Your phone keeps it.
  • Badges. Rare moments earn badges: Moon Landing, Three Bagger, Clean Sweep, Great Minds, Pickpocket. Your phone keeps those too.
  • Good to know. Bet on your own caption to bluff — it scores nothing. Captions are tidied the same way, the real one too. The real one isn’t always the funniest. Tap the cartoon to zoom.
