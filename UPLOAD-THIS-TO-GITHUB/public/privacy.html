Privacy, briefly.

It’s a party game. It keeps as little as it can, for as short as it can, and it doesn’t sell anything — least of all you.

What the game server holds

  • Names you type to join, the captions you write, and your bets and scores.
  • All of it sits in the server’s memory, only for that game room, seen only by the players in it. It’s never written to disk.
  • A room is deleted soon after the last phone leaves (10 minutes from the lobby, 30 from a game), and the server forgets every room whenever it restarts.
  • To stop people guessing room codes, it counts wrong codes from each network for a few minutes, in memory.
  • It also keeps a running count for the game’s owner (games played, players per game, whether people joined by QR code, link or typed code, and Gallery cards shared): numbers only, no names, no captions, nothing that points to you.

What stays on your phone

  • Your name, The Gallery of framed captions, your rating, and which cartoons you’ve seen.
  • While you play, a copy of tonight’s game (names, captions, scores), so the game can come back if the server restarts. It’s dropped after a few hours.
  • It lives in your browser’s storage on that phone, nowhere else. Your name is also kept in a small cookie, so the phone still knows it if the browser tidies its storage. Clearing the site’s data in your browser settings erases all of it.
  • When you join a game, your phone shares your Gallery with that room so the table can see it.

Photos, and kids

  • The game takes no photos. It never asks for your camera, and nobody’s picture is kept anywhere.
  • Kids are welcome to play. Nobody needs a real name, an email, or an account.

What we don’t do

  • No ads.
  • No tracking or analytics. The only cookie is your name, on your own phone.
  • No selling or sharing your names or captions. With anyone. Ever.
  • No accounts, no emails, no passwords.

The fine print, in large print

  • The game’s lettering comes from Google Fonts, so your phone asks Google for the font files.
  • Like any website, the company that hosts the game may keep ordinary visit logs (like your network address) for a short time.

Questions?

Ask the game’s owner — the person who shared Closed Caption with you. Want something deleted that you can’t delete yourself? They’ll take care of it.
