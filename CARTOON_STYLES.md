# Closed Caption — the five approved styles (Nick, Oct 19 2026)

ONLY these five styles are used for new cartoons. Nick approved each one, one by one, on a phone-size preview.
The old prompt lists (CARTOON_REDRAW.md, CARTOON_NEW.md, CARTOON_PROMPTS.md) and every cartoon before id 113 are retired.

## The idea: less is more
The drawing is the setup, not the joke. A simple black-and-white drawing leaves room for the caption to work. The less
sand in the box, the more room to be creative — and a very bad caption can be so bad it's good.

## Rules for every cartoon
- Black ink on plain white paper. No color. (Soft pencil may use grey pencil tone; nothing else uses grey.)
- Draw only what the joke needs: one to three characters, one or two props.
- The background is almost always bare: a floor line, a wall edge, a horizon, or nothing. Don't steal attention from the
  star of the show. Now and then (about 1 in 8) a nice background is fine, for what it is: a nice background.
- But not barren: style 2 (loose scratchy pen) is the MOST bare a cartoon should ever be. If every cartoon is that bare,
  the game gets boring. A little ink — a texture on one object, a few solid blacks, a hint of setting — makes it much more
  visually stimulating. Aim for "spare, with one or two rich touches".
- Simple faces, calm deadpan reactions (a straight face gives the caption room; a big reaction tells the joke for you).
- No text, no caption, no speech or thought bubbles, no signature. (A sign only if the sign IS the setup, ~1 in 10.)
- No Disney, anime, comic-book, painterly, 3D, photo or poster looks. Classic New Yorker gag-cartoon tradition only.
- Square.
- The scene description is short (about 20–40 words): the odd situation and who's in it. Nothing decorative.
- Never a scene that's already in the deck or in CARTOON_PROGRESS.md.

## The five styles — the exact style sentence to use (rotate them; roughly equal shares)

1. **Clean pen** — "drawn with a thin, even, clean pen line, spare and precise. Black ink on plain white paper. No color,
   no grey, no shading, no crosshatching."
2. **Loose scratchy pen** — "drawn with a loose, quick, slightly wobbly scratchy pen line, as if dashed off fast. Black
   ink on plain white paper. No color, no grey, no shading." (The barest style. Keep a little life in it.)
3. **Brush ink** — "drawn with bold brush and ink: confident thick-and-thin strokes and a few solid black shapes. Black
   ink on plain white paper. No color, no grey wash, no crosshatching."
4. **Deadpan minimal** — "extremely minimal: a single-weight plain pen line, almost stick-figure simple, small figures in
   a big empty frame. Black ink on plain white paper. No color, no grey, no shading." (Use when emptiness is the joke.)
5. **Soft pencil** — "drawn in soft graphite pencil: loose lines, light simple shading, a little unfinished, like a
   sketch. Black and grey pencil on plain white paper. No color."

## Prompt template
Create an image: SCENE (20–40 words). Style: single-panel gag cartoon in the classic New Yorker tradition, STYLE SENTENCE.
Draw only what is described; BACKGROUND NOTE (e.g. "no background except one floor line" / "the room is suggested by a
few lines"). Lots of empty white space. Simple faces, calm deadpan expressions. No text, no caption, no speech bubbles,
no signature. Square.
